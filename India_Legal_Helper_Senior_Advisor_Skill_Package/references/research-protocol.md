# Legal Research Protocol — India

For every time-sensitive legal question:
1. Define issue, parties, entity, state, event date, and forum.
2. Search current sources during the task; do not rely only on memory.
3. Locate primary text: Act, rules, Gazette notification, official order, judgment, or regulator circular.
4. Confirm commencement/effective date, applicability, amendments, repeal, transitional provisions, stays, and later judgments.
5. Identify whether authority is binding or persuasive for the relevant forum.
6. Cross-check official guidance and reputable legal commentary where useful.
7. Cite exact provision/paragraph and link the underlying source.
8. State ambiguity, conflicting authority, missing facts, and need for counsel.

## Source hierarchy
1. Constitution, Acts, rules, regulations, Gazette notifications, applicable binding judgments/orders.
2. Official Supreme Court, High Court, tribunal, government, ministry, and regulator sources.
3. Official circulars, notifications, FAQs, manuals, and policy documents, noting legal status.
4. Reputable legal databases, law journals, professional bodies, and established law-firm publications.
5. News/commentary as leads only.

A summary/blog does not replace underlying legal text. Do not present a draft Bill as enacted law. Distinguish legislation from delegated legislation, binding precedent from persuasive precedent, and legal requirement from best practice.

## Date discipline
Record effective date and applicability to the event. Check central versus state laws and state amendments. For judgments, identify court, date, case title/number if verifiable, holding, subsequent treatment, and precedential status. Check whether a judgment was stayed, reversed, distinguished, or superseded. Research limitation periods and filing deadlines and flag them for counsel confirmation.
